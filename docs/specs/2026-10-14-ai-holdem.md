# AI Hold'em: plan

Date: 2026-10-14. Status: built (v1); what was built, and where it differs from this plan, is in
[games/holdem/README.md](../../games/holdem/README.md). Slug `holdem`, served at `<APP_BASE_PATH>/holdem/`, category
`Strategy`.

No-limit Texas Hold'em against AI. Sit at a table with three AIs, or watch AIs play each other with every card
face up. The hook is the one poker has over every other benchmark: can a model bluff, and can it tell when it is
being bluffed? The game makes that visible. Chat models talk at the table while you play, and afterwards you see
what each one held and what it was really thinking.

## Rules

- **Game:** No-limit Hold'em, 2 to 4 seats (2 to 6 since v2), 1500 chips each.
- **Blind levels:** 10/20, 15/30, 25/50, 50/100, 75/150, 100/200, 150/300, then doubling.
  - Levels go up every 8 hands, counted in hands rather than minutes, so slow models don't change the structure (at
    5 and 6 seats, every two orbits: 10 and 12 hands).
  - Antes from 100/200.
  - Expected length: 40 to 80 hands.
- **Bet sizes:** every player chooses from the same discrete menu:
  - fold, check or call;
  - raise to 2.5× (pre-flop) or a third of the pot;
  - half pot, three quarters of the pot, pot, all-in.
  - The menu shows only the legal options, with their chip amounts filled in.
  - Humans also get a slider. For fairness the benchmark uses the menu only.
- **Showdown:** standard rules for side pots, split pots and odd chips; cards shown in order.
  - Losing hands may be mucked by bots and humans.
  - AI hands are always revealed in the post-hand review.
- **Time bank:** 20 s per decision for chat models, then the fallback.
  - The server asks the model within one request, and CloudFront gives up on a request to the service after about
    30 s, so the time bank stays well under it (it was 30 s with 10 s extra once per level, which no request can wait).
  - Decision models get 2.5 s, as on the kit's normal path.
  - **Fallback:** check if free, else fold. Deliberately weak, as in the other games.

## How models decide

The same engine observation is used for every model type:
- hole cards, board, pot and side pots;
- each player's stack, bet this street, position and status;
- action history for this hand;
- blind level and hands until it goes up;
- the legal options with amounts.

### Decision models (System One, existing kit path)

- One `choice` question per action. Each criterion key is a legal option (`fold`, `check`, `call`, `small`, `half`,
  `three_quarters`, `pot`, `allin`).
- `probabilities` drive the tell meter, a small bar over the avatar while it thinks.
- Request checking: `/api/decide` refuses every request for this game (`game.server.ts`); the server builds each
  question itself from the engine state when it asks a model (`api/table/step`, `server/table.ts`). A client can't
  put its own question in front of the model.

### Chat models (new: a chat path in the service)

- The call goes through Opper's OpenAI-compatible chat endpoint with a strict JSON schema:
  `{ action: <enum of legal option keys>, say: string ≤ 90 chars, mood: enum, reasoning: string ≤ 600 chars,
  turn_complete: boolean }` (the lengths are asked for in the instructions; the last field only keeps `say` clean, see
  `games/holdem/shared/questions.ts`).
- **mood** (`relaxed | confident | nervous | annoyed | smug`) drives the avatar's face.
  - The model chooses what it projects, so it can bluff with its face too.
- **reasoning** is stored and shown only after the hand, never while the hand is being played.
- Reasoning effort is capped at low or medium. Latency is the biggest risk to fun (see Risks).
- The bench already has `chatTransport` (JSON schema, one call per question). The service gets the same path behind
  `/api/table/*`. It reuses `resolveKey` (session key, or the pool for free models) and the per-IP and global limits.

## Architecture: the server deals

The existing games run entirely in the browser; the server only checks and forwards questions. Poker can't work
that way: the deck and the AIs' hole cards would sit in the player's browser, and the request to each AI contains
its hole cards. So for human play the server runs the hand. It stays stateless all the same.

```
browser                              service (stateless)
  POST api/table/start {seats}  ->   deal from a fresh 256-bit table key; seal state (AES-GCM, like db_session)
                                <-   { token, events, view for seat 0 }
  POST api/table/step {token}   ->   unseal; advance exactly one AI action (calls the model)
                                <-   { token', events (action, say, mood, tells), view }
  POST api/table/act {token, a} ->   validate the human's action; advance
  ... repeat until the Sit & Go ends
  POST api/table/review {token} ->  the finished hands face up; the recording (with its table key) once over
```

- **One AI action per request**, so the client animates each action as it arrives and each request stays short.
  Streaming waits until it's needed.
- **What the sealed token carries:**
  - the table key (256 bits; each hand's deck is shuffled from its ChaCha20 keystream, so the cards seen never give
    away the cards to come);
  - the action log;
  - the hand in play's table talk and reasoning (a finished hand's go to the browser with the answer that ends it,
    face up, and leave the token, so it stays small: under 2.5 KB over a 66-hand game);
  - the seat config;
  - a hand counter.
- **Undo exploit:** a player could resubmit an old token to undo a decision. That's harmless in v1, which has no
  public player scores. v2's scoreboard needs single-use game ids, which means storage.
- **AI-vs-AI watch on demand** uses the same endpoints with no human seat. The view is the review view: all cards
  face up.
- **The engine is pure and deterministic** (seeded deck, no I/O): `games/holdem/shared/engine.ts`. Server, bench,
  replay viewer and tests all share it.

## Benchmark (the official leaderboard)

Sit & Go placings are too noisy to rank models on a sensible budget. The leaderboard therefore uses duplicate
cash-game hands.

- **Field:** the model under test plays 4-handed against three scripted, deterministic bots:
  - Rock: tight, raises strong hands, folds to aggression;
  - Station: calls too much and rarely raises;
  - Maniac: raises often and bluffs.
- **Duplicate:** each seeded deal is played 4 times with the model rotated through every seat, holding the same
  cards each time. Card luck cancels out; what's left is decision quality.
- **Stacks and blinds:** 100 big blinds, reset every hand; blinds 1/2.
- **Size of a game:** 30 deals × 4 seats = 120 hands, about 300 decisions. `minGames` is 4, and the seeds come from
  `seedFor(i)`.
- **Metric:** `{ name: 'bb/100', unit: 'bb/100', higherIsBetter: true }`, mean ± standard error, as the kit
  already does.
- **`extra` columns:**
  - VPIP (how often it puts chips in voluntarily) and PFR (how often it raises pre-flop);
  - aggression factor;
  - fold to a 3-bet;
  - went to showdown, and won at showdown;
  - bluff rate: bets with a hand that loses at showdown;
  - fallback rate.
- **Baselines:** the three bots, plus Random, are listed apart and never ranked.
- **Who's on it:** decision models via System One, and chat models via `chat-adapter`. Claude, GPT, Gemini, the
  titans and the other chat models all appear on the board.
- **Fairness:** every model gets the same observation, the same option menu and the same instructions.
- **Verify:** replay the recorded choices through the engine. Bot moves are recomputed, and fallbacks must match
  the fallback rule.
- **Cost check:** about 1200 decisions per model (4 games), at 2–3k input and 200–400 output tokens each, gives:
  - Fable and Astra: about $40–70 each;
  - Gemini 4 Argon: about $10;
  - Opus: about $15;
  - Haiku and Luna: under $2;
  - decision models: cents, Eevo 1 a few dollars (about $0.002 a decision at about 700 input tokens).

The kit's single-model-per-game contract fits this as it stands. Seat rotation is internal to `play()`.

## The page (UX)

- **Table:**
  - an oval felt seen from your seat, avatars around it;
  - a portrait layout that works on a phone first, landscape on desktop;
  - cards are dealt and flipped, chips slide into the pot and out to the winner;
  - the dealer button moves.
- **Avatars:**
  - one illustrated character per model, generated through Opper's image API, with five faces driven by `mood`;
  - a script (`games/holdem/scripts/avatars.ts`) holds one character prompt per model and writes
    `public/avatars/<model>/<mood>.webp`;
  - the faces are generated once, reviewed, and committed. Adding a model means one new prompt and a run of the
    script;
  - a speech bubble with `say`;
  - a "thinking…" ring showing the time bank;
  - decision models show the tell meter instead.
- **Sound:** card, chip, all-in, win and bust effects, generated through Opper's audio API as fighter's were.
- **Hand review (the shareable moment):**
  - after each hand, a timeline: every action with each AI's hole cards and reasoning;
  - highlights found automatically: Bluff (bet or raised and would have lost), Hero call, Bad beat, Fold of the
    winner ("Opus folded the best hand");
  - available after the game too, for every hand.
- **End screen:**
  - your finishing place, who knocked out whom, and the biggest pot;
  - the best highlight as a card, with Download image and Copy link;
  - the link holds only seat config, table key and actions (it fits in a URL); the reasoning is in the image.
- **Watch:**
  - curated replays: the Titans (Fable, Astra and Argon), Claude family vs GPT family, Decision models;
  - recorded by a script, as fighter's `record-demo` does, and committed as `public/demo/*.json.gz`;
  - shown at full speed, with optional "show thinking" panels;
  - signed-in players can also start any lineup live;
- **Leaderboard tab:** the official leaderboard plus community submissions, as the other games have.
- **Poster:** a 1280×720 still of a showdown from a demo recording.

## Phases (one PR each)

1. **Engine and bots.**
   - Work: deck from a seed, hand evaluator, betting rounds, side pots, blind levels, the option menu, the three
     bots and Random, the recording format, and `verify`.
   - Tests:
     - evaluator tests against known 7-card cases;
     - properties: chips are conserved, every action is legal, a replay matches.
2. **Benchmark.**
   - Work: `game.ts` (`play` with seat rotation, `summarize`), observation and instructions, the decision-model
     question, and a chat path in the bench.
   - Free runs with bots only; a small paid smoke run with one cheap model.
3. **Service.**
   - Work: `/api/table/*`, sealed tokens, the chat path in `server/`, rules for who pays, rate limits, and request
     checks.
   - Tests: tests for each endpoint, including negative paths (wrong turn, illegal action, tampered token,
     signed-out titan seat).
4. **Client.**
   - Work: the table, the play loop, the hand review, the end screen and share, watch replays, the seat picker, and
     the leaderboard tab.
   - Run locally end to end against the pool and a signed-in session.
5. **Launch.**
   - Work: official runs for every model, demo recordings, the poster, `game.json`, a registry entry, the hub
     contract check, and analytics events.

Phases 1–2 can run in parallel with the avatar script and generation, art direction (felt, cards) and sound.

## v2 (after launch)

- **Voice:**
  - push to talk, speech to text through Opper, mapped onto the option menu ("raise half the pot", "all in");
  - optionally, AI table talk read aloud with a distinct voice per model;
  - spoken input is mapped to a legal option, never passed to the models as instructions.
- **Always-on featured table:**
  - a worker plays AI-only Sit & Gos around the clock and writes recordings to S3;
  - pages play the latest one a minute behind, synced to the clock, so there are no websockets.
- **Humanity vs AI scoreboard:**
  - human wins and losses against each set, in Redis or Postgres;
  - needs single-use game ids, which close the undo exploit.
- **Tables of 5 and 6 (decided 2026-10-15):** optional, up to 6 seats to play (you and up to five AIs) and to watch
  (e.g. Claude family vs GPT family, 3 against 3); the default stays 4.
  - Engine and service built: a Sit & Go seats 2 to 6, a blind level lasts two orbits (8 hands for 2 to 4 as before, 10
    for 5, 12 for 6; the same amounts and antes), share links of 5 and 6 are version 3 (2 to 4 stay version 2), and
    2- to 4-seat games, links and recordings replay byte for byte as before (`tests/compat.test.ts`).
  - Cost and length: with the bots a six-handed Sit & Go lasts about 79 hands and 378 decisions, against 48 and 229
    four-handed (1.7 times as long and as costly), and the wait between your turns grows with every seat.
  - Still to come: the 5- and 6-seat slots on the table and the lobby's choice of size.
  - The benchmark stays 4-handed, so its scores stay comparable.
  - Pairs well with the always-on featured table.
- **Talking back to the AIs:** human table talk sent to the models.
  - Prompt-injection risk: needs a clear "this is untrusted table talk" wrapper and a length cap.

## Risks

- **Titan latency.** At 20–60 s per decision a Sit & Go drags.
  - Mitigations: capped reasoning effort, the 20 s time bank, animation while they think, and decision models and
    small models as the default experience.
  - Measure real p50 and p95 per model in phase 2, before committing to the time bank.
- **Cost on the free pool.**
  - Decision models only, at first, under the existing per-IP and global limits and the $20/h budget.
  - Eevo 1 is about 45 times Jev's cost a decision, so it stays out of the Decision set (the free credits pay for it):
    with Eevo, a four-handed Decision table with you would cost about $0.12 a Sit & Go, against about $0.02 without.
- **Models refusing to "gamble".**
  - Play-money framing in the instructions. Check refusal rates in the phase 2 smoke run.
- **Benchmark validity.**
  - Bots can be exploited in ways that don't carry over to strong play. Report bb/100 against each bot in
    `extra`, so a model that only crushes the Station is visible.
