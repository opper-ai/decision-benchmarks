# Jev Fighter (then called Stickfight) v2: a harness that measures decisions

Date: 2026-10-06. Follows four independent reviews of v1 (prompt design, mechanics, methodology,
baselines). Their findings, in short: the option facts handed models the answer (a text parser beat
every model 132-0), a knocked-down fighter could never defend (one knockdown = KO), the second mover
always won, real time mostly measured latency, the greedy fallback played for slow models, and the
sample was too small and too repetitive to rank anything.

## Goals

1. Winning should require a decision: no option text that names the answer.
2. Every situation should have counterplay (no unblockable loops, no free reactions).
3. Measure decision quality first (paused), speed second (real time), against baselines, with
   confidence intervals.

## Engine (ENGINE_VERSION 3)

- **Wake-up.** A fighter getting up is actionable on the last frame of getup (still invulnerable),
  so its move starts before any timed attack can connect. Throw protection: a fighter cannot be
  thrown for 6 frames after getup, hitstun or blockstun.
- **Chip damage.** Blocked strikes deal a quarter of their damage (rounded down). Waiting is no
  longer free.
- **Uppercut** knocks down on any hit (it was -3 on hit, useless outside anti-air).
- **High kick** reach 1.3 and recovery 20 (it dominated 1.3 to 1.4 and was safe on block there).
- **Special** really dashes: moves 1.0 forward during startup, reach 1.1 from where it lands; armor
  only during startup.
- **Combos** are guaranteed: a chain starter that hits applies only light pushback.
- **Variety.** The seed sets the start distance (2.0 to 4.0) and position (anywhere it fits).

## Reaction time

Both modes describe the opponent as it was 10 ticks (167 ms) ago, roughly human reaction. A move
the opponent started less than 10 ticks ago is not visible yet. Fast moves (jab, throw, low kick)
cannot be reacted to; slow ones (sweep, high kick, special, jump kick) can.

## Modes

- **Paused (the main score).** A fighter is asked only when it is actionable, about the exact
  current state (with the reaction lag above). The fight waits for every answer. Fighters free on
  the same tick decide simultaneously.
- **Real time (the speed track).** As v1: asked ahead by measured latency, the fight never waits.

## Tracks

Assisted is the default everywhere. Raw is an advanced option (a disclosure on the Fight page,
`--track raw` in the bench) with its own leaderboard.

- **Assisted.** Each option gets a payoff row: the HP swing (+ dealt, - taken) if the opponent
  replies with each of a few plausible actions started on the same tick (guard, jab, low kick, throw,
  plus their recent habit), and the frames until you recover. If the opponent is visibly committed,
  one column: against what they are doing. No single option reads as "the answer"; choosing means
  weighing risk and reading the opponent.
- **Raw.** The situation and the rules only. Each option is the move's static description.

The rules and move table are stated once in the instructions. Option order is shuffled per question
(seeded) and recorded. Target length: raw about 350 tokens, assisted about 550; models with a short
context drop payoff columns until the question fits.

## Fallback

A missing, late or invalid answer, or a move no longer legal, plays `block_high` (or keeps the
current block). The harness never plays well on a model's behalf.

## Outcomes and stats

- A decision's outcome window runs from when it is applied until the fighter is next actionable
  (waiting time is not credited to it).
- Decisions record when the answer arrived.
- Leaderboard per track and mode, with Bradley-Terry ratings and bootstrap 95% intervals, plus a
  pairwise win matrix. Elo is dropped.
- Calibration is reported as a Brier skill score against the model's own base rate (0 = no better
  than always guessing its average), not raw Brier.

## Baselines

Built-in players that appear on every leaderboard: `baseline/random` (uniform over offered moves),
`baseline/guard` (always block high), `baseline/greedy` (best payoff against the opponent's most
likely reply, from the engine's own simulation) and `baseline/payoff` (best average over the assisted
payoff row; assisted track only).

## Success criteria

- No baseline wins every match; random and guard lose to greedy; the knockdown loop is gone.
- A model must beat random with its interval clear of 0.5 to be called better than random.
- Re-run independent reviews of v2 before trusting the leaderboard.

## Addenda (after the v2 re-review)

- Payoff rows simulate an opponent that replies as soon as it can act (punishes count) and is seen
  through a reaction delay with extrapolation (`shared/view.ts`). Columns: stand, worst (named), usual;
  `adv` = frame advantage. Baselines read only what the question shows. Added `baseline/maximin`.
- Engine v4: blocks are held 18 frames. Engine v5: passivity rule on by default (8 s free without
  attacking or stepping in costs 1 HP/s; time under attack does not count). Verified on the baseline
  round robin: it fires only in mutual standoffs, never against an opponent that engages.
- Ratings: Bradley-Terry iterated to convergence, centred on the pool mean, bootstrap stratified by
  pairing. Calibration skill dropped from the leaderboard (not a forecast of winning).
- Bench version 2 (2026-10-13): the benchmark plays live, real time on the raw track, not paused on the assisted one.
  The fight runs at its normal speed as on the page: the model is asked ahead (`askAhead`, by its measured latency),
  stale questions are refreshed when a fresh answer can still arrive in time, an answer plays on the first frame its
  fighter can act after it arrives, and one later than 2 s (the page's live default and the server's call limit) is
  dropped, its call cancelled, and the fallback blocks. The gauntlet baselines answered at once on the frame their
  fighter could act and on the raw track work out the assisted payoff rows from the state. The verifier replays the recording frame by frame from the
  ticks each answer was applied on; the timing (when an answer arrived) is self-reported in a submission.
- Bench version 3 (2026-10-13): the baselines think as on the page (`BASELINE_THINK_MS`, 250 ms), counted as 15
  frames (`BOT_DELAY_TICKS`): asked like a model (ahead, refreshed when stale), their answer arrives 15 frames after
  the question (`ScriptedPlayer`, used by the live match and the verifier alike). The seeded random baseline joins the
  gauntlet as a fourth opponent, the floor; 104 official games (13 fights x 4 baselines x 2 sides).
